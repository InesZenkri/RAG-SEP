Mitochondria are double-membrane organelles found in most eukaryotic cells.
They produce most of the cell's ATP through cellular respiration. The inner
membrane is folded into structures called cristae which increase the surface
area available for ATP synthesis.

Cellular respiration in mitochondria proceeds through glycolysis, the citric
acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation. Oxidative phosphorylation takes
place along the electron transport chain embedded in the inner membrane.

Mitochondria contain their own circular DNA and ribosomes, and they replicate
independently of the nucleus. Most mitochondrial DNA is inherited from the
mother in animals.
